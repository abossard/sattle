# How the shop gets its health model

Each chapter starts with something that happened in the recorded shop. A full-size figure explains how it happened, then names the principle that carries over to another system. The opening pulls back from one checkout path to the entire recorded master graph. The vocabulary and rules can be maintained by people. The figures show what Sattle can derive from those definitions and from evidence.

The listener asks a question about the figure. The guide answers from the shop’s records and explains the concept. Show the question while the listener speaks, then hold the term and its definition during the reply. The film and interactive guide share the drawings and the local Kokoro recording.

The shop is the bundled pg-shop replay. The cache chapters compare controlled edits to copies of that recording. Keep the settings, readings and rules that each experiment holds fixed visible when comparing outcomes.

The main film has four chapters. The first follows the illustrated shop from source records to five views. The second opens with a shop operator asking when the trouble began. Show the 10:10 and 10:11 UTC database checks, trace PostgreSQL to the webshop, and walk through the recovery rule. The third asks how a health model keeps up when the system changes. Compare two pg-shop runs with the same rules and one extra Hubble flow. Show Redis appearing under the API in the health model while web keeps its existing branch to that same cache. The added branch can be traced to its call, endpoint and DNS records. The fourth follows the executable deployment-health GKQL experiment. Its drawn pattern matches pods to deployments. Two pod calls produce one workload edge. [Sources](../foundry/sources.md)

## Visual language

Both guides and films use Sattle Canvas’s dark drawing sheet. Graphite is the drawing area. A slightly lighter surface holds controls and evidence. Thin rules separate sections. Use the system sans serif for questions, labels and explanations, and monospace for identifiers, times and revision numbers. [Canvas design](../../../canvas/DESIGN.md) · [Shared palette](theme.css)

Begin the master graph in grayscale. Introduce color when the model can say what a mark means. Pink identifies a match, blue marks a selection or projection, and mint with a plus sign marks an addition in a revision. Pair health colors with their names. Healthy is green, Degraded is amber, Unhealthy is coral and Unknown is gray.

The chapter heading and definition share a color in both the walkthrough and the film. Teal marks the master graph, violet marks the Health Tree, pink marks system changes, and amber marks the GKQL program. Within the drawings, color the groups, paths and state panels that the guide describes. Keep their labels legible against the dark fills.
