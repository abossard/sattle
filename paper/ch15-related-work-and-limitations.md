**Status.** Text only.
**Gate.** All: the limits table of 15.2 is sorted by gate.
Uses: all chapters. Sources: [R01] section 2.5 and 2.4, [R03] section 1, [R02]; every source below was opened by R01, R02, R03 or this paper (see [references.md](references.md)).

## 15.1 Related work

Each entry states what Sattle takes and what it does not.

**Incremental computation.**
- DBSP and Z-sets give the algebra, the classification of operators (linear, bilinear, non-linear) and the incrementalization theorem used in Ch. 4, 6 and 13 ([budiu2023]). Sattle does not treat group-element deltas as valid graph states (Theorem 4.9).
- Differential dataflow indexes differences by partially ordered versions, the reference answer for incremental recursion with retractions ([mcsherry2013]). Sattle restricts recursion to positive monotone rules over a finite domain (Theorem 6.5) and re-derives affected keys on retraction.
- Timely dataflow and Naiad are the origin of the term frontier ([murray2013]). The engine's frontier shares its partial-order meaning for source partitions only (Def. 6.6).
- Incremental view maintenance with counting and delete-and-rederive ([gupta1993]). Counting fails on cyclic derivations (Theorem 6.6(b)).
- The Dataflow model gives windows, watermarks and late data ([akidau2015]). Sattle's windows close by explicit logical time, never by the wall clock (Def. 7.6).
- Quine standing queries give positive and negative match results and the invariant "integrated incremental output equals batch" that Sattle uses as a conformance test (O6.1).

**Streaming and temporal graph systems.** Memgraph separates stream transport from transformation and states that batching does not imply exactly-once ([R01] G5). Raphtory is a temporal graph analysis system ([steer2020]); it is about analysis, not replayable derivation. Streaming model transformations by complex event processing ([david2016]). Sattle differs by its recorded-input determinism (Theorem 6.3) and by typed definitions in the graph.

**Graph schemas, ontologies and meta-models.**
- PG-Schema ([pgschema]) and PG-Keys ([pgkeys2021]) are the closest prior art for typed property graphs and keys: Sattle takes declaration shape, abstract and closed types, and key constraints, and drops loose validation. GQL (ISO/IEC 39075:2024, [gql]) and Cypher patterns ([cypher-patterns], [opencypher]) give pattern vocabulary. Sattle's `Match` has no variable-length `*` paths and no imperative clauses.
- RDFS, OWL 2 and SHACL ([rdfs], [owl2], [shacl]) separate vocabulary from closed-world validation. Sattle follows SHACL for validation reports and "shapes for shapes", and does not use inference.
- MOF and Ecore ([mof], [emf]) are the model for a self-describing top level with reflective access (Ch. 2). Sattle has three levels and one containment edge.
- The property graph model ([angles2018]) requires edge endpoints to be nodes. Sattle's graph is partial (Def. 3.2).
- Kubernetes CRDs ([k8s-crd]), GraphQL type extension ([graphql]), Backstage ([backstage]) and Dynatrace Smartscape ([smartscape]) contribute runtime-loaded types, versions, qualified references, key-based ids and static versus time-bound edges. Custom kinds as code, conversion webhooks and opaque ids are not taken.
- Ontology change management ([mahfoudh2015]) and functorial data migration ([spivak2012]) inform the compatibility classes and migrations of Ch. 2. Sattle's migrations are projections, and the compatibility table is a structural classifier, not a categorical construction. Data exchange with value invention ([fagin2005]) is related to cross-ontology projections. Sattle forbids fresh identities inside fixpoints (Ch. 5), which avoids chase-termination issues.

**Bidirectional transformations.** Asymmetric lenses ([foster2007]), symmetric lenses and complements ([hofmann2011]), constant complement ([bancilhon1981]), delta lenses ([diskin2011]), lenses for graph transformations ([hidaka2010]), bijective lenses ([miltner2018]). Triple graph grammars ([anjorin2016]), conditions for correct and complete derived operations ([hermann2013]), multi-version TGGs ([barkowsky2024]), multi-view consistency ([gottmann2016]). Sattle takes laws as declared obligations, derives backward steps from combinators, and restricts `sync` to a v1 fragment without negative application conditions (Ch. 8). Arbitrary user-written `put` functions are not accepted. Graph transformation as software architecture reconfiguration is in [wermelinger2002].

**Provenance and patches.** Provenance semirings ([green2007]) unify support, lineage and Z-set weights (Ch. 9). Categorical patch theory ([mimram2013]) frames patches (Ch. 4); Sattle's patches are guarded and form an inverse category (Proposition 4.5). CRDTs ([shapiro2011]) contrast commutative merge with ordered patches (Ch. 4.5).

**Time and consistency.** SQL:2011 separates system and application time ([kulkarni2012]); Sattle indexes system time only (Ch. 3). Consistent snapshots ([chandy1985]) motivate the aligned-frontier mode that remains open (Def. 6.6).

**Storage and scale.** Chapter 11 cites the storage, partitioning and vectorization literature ([gonzalez2012], [stanton2012], [zhu2020], [deleo2021], [boncz2005], [pelkonen2015], [mcsherry2020], [berkholz2017], [henzinger2015]) and the Azure documentation.

**Health models.** Azure Monitor health models define components, signals with thresholds and worst-of roll-up; the worked example of Ch. 7 (7.5) follows that vocabulary as a stack of projections. The paper cites the vendor documentation only through the engine README and the definitions, and does not evaluate Azure Monitor itself.

**AI-assisted operations.** Recent work on LLM and agent support for incident handling and AIOps ([R01] verified entries: ActionNex, arXiv 2604.03512, 2026; a 2026 survey in IEEE TNSM; log analysis with LLMs, IJACSA 2025) is out of scope. Sattle's ADR-041, 043 and 044 on AI rule generation appear in no contract and no chapter (finding 31).

## 15.2 Limitations and non-goals

**What the prototype does not do, and why.** One list, so that no claim of this paper rests on one of these by silence. The table is sorted by the gate of the research plan that each line belongs to (README, "Research plan and gates"). Each line is a fact about the engine at evaluation 12 or the canvas at the canvas of evaluation 11; the reason is what the engine README, the protocol, set B (runs of the project that the authors did not repeat; Ch. 14, 14.1) or the chapter gives.

| Gate | Not done | Why | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | The image of a level checked at ingest (Ch. 5, 5.8) | The checker requires the mapping of a `lift` to be declared a function or the statement to carry an `images` policy. `Realizes` is declared `0 .. 1` per source; conformance reports a second image and an edit that adds one is rejected, but ingestion does not check cardinality, so recorded data can hold two, and the lift then leaves that service out. The service level says `images each`, so a pod that backs two services counts for both and conservation does not hold for it | a cardinality check on ingested rows (O5.8) |
| 1a | Three rulings of the paper that the engine does not implement (Ch. 6, 7; D34 to D36) | The definitions were ruled after the reference evaluator showed that the paper did not determine the result. Not built: telemetry windows combined by the property's rule on an identity merge (the engine adds for every property, right for `sum` only); retention of telemetry windows as a function of the logical frontier and a declared age (the store drops 48 h behind the newest window it has seen); conflicts listed with the commit (they are counted per run) | a retention declaration in the language and the store port; the combine rule in the merge; a conflict list in the commit record |
| 1a | The lowering of `lift` shown correct in general (Ch. 5, 5.8) | The statement has no evaluation rule of its own; its properties are those of the operators it is lowered to. That the lowering implements Def. 5.17 and 5.18 is tested by equality with hand-written statements on three inputs and by one case per clause, not proved and not tested on generated graphs | a property test that compares a lift with the definitions directly |
| 1b | A stated claim of what the language and the meta-graph can and cannot express | The paper lists what was built (operator kinds, `lift`, definitions as data, two health models and a report without engine code) and nowhere states the class of transformations this covers, what lies outside it, or what needs an engine release beyond the closed kernel. The word did not occur in the paper before the research plan | the investigation `paper/research/05-expressiveness-and-flexibility.md`, then claims with evidence in Ch. 2 and Ch. 5 |
| 1b | Rule class `sync` (Ch. 8, D12) | The engine has no stored correspondence graph and no FWD and BWD translators; the four other classes (`forward`, `readonly`, `bijective`, `lens`) cover every rule of the definitions. `/palette` lists `sync` as unsupported and a use is a typed error at its position. Theorem 8.7 (T12) has no engine evidence | the correspondence graph, the translators and a worked example that needs them |
| 1b | Operator `Delta` (Ch. 5, D11) | No property in the definitions or in the captures is cumulative, so it would ship without data to test it on. `/palette` lists it as unsupported | a cumulative source and a rule that reads it as a difference |
| 1c | Budgets per node, edge and telemetry point, met at 1 million master nodes on one machine | Ch. 13 states hypotheses for a reference estate of 750,000 entities on Azure hardware with Cosmos DB, not budgets per element. The hot graph takes about 100 bytes per element (H9, one run), but the whole engine holds 5.4 GB at the end of the estate run, and a window closes in 1.5 to 1.7 s at 240,000 pairs. No run has 1 million master nodes, and no store was measured at that size | the investigation `paper/research/04-compute-and-memory-efficiency.md`, a measured baseline, stated budgets, then runs against them on more than one store |
| 1c | A real Cosmos account with RU figures (Ch. 11, 11.12) | No Azure deployment of the engine's store exists. The emulator reports a charge of 1 per request, so request counts are exact and RU, latency, throttling and region effects are not measured. H3, H5 and H7 on Cosmos are open, and H7 has only emulator figures, with two different labels from two sets of runs | an account in the engine's region with provisioned throughput, the harness with `--store cosmos=`, the RU charge per commit |
| 1c | Bulk execution in the Cosmos SDK (Ch. 11, 11.12) | Not tried. The batched writes make one transactional batch per touched element partition, about 1.06 requests per element at head, which is the floor of that design because a batch cannot span partitions; bulk execution would merge requests across the partitions of one physical partition. Without RU figures there is no way to say what it would save | a real account and the RU charge of both designs on the same capture |
| 1c | A reference machine with no other load (Ch. 13, 13.6) | Every ten-run figure was taken while other contributors ran on the machine (load average 14 to 115). The statuses of 13.7 are stated for that configuration; a pass under load is conservative for an upper-bound target and a failure is weak evidence | the harness of `engine/PERF.md` on the lab laptop with nothing else running, and on the hardware of Def. 13.4 for Cosmos |
| 2a | Real-capture results reproducible from the repository (Ch. 14) | The two real captures are gitignored and stay local, and the tracked space file carries invented names (the real workload arguments are in a gitignored overlay next to them). The fixtures in the repository were rebuilt from the first capture with a stricter scrubber and the `estate` fixture is synthetic, so a reader can reproduce the fixture and `estate` results and not the real-capture ones, which rest on `paper/evidence/*.txt` and on the claim that the captures exist | publish a scrubbed capture, or keep the evidence files as the record |
| 2a | Capabilities on a real capture (Ch. 5, 5.8) | Capabilities are authored data. The two real captures recorded none, so the capability level is empty on them and exists only on the synthetic `estate` | a capture with `authoring` records |
| 2b | Complete lineage through windowed operators (Ch. 9, 9.5) | Lineage names every source for row-level emits and for a `Group` without windows. For an element emitted from a windowed `Group`, a `Rollup`, a `Threshold` or a `Series`, and for rows reduced to scalars by `select` before the emit, it holds the elements of the group key and says `complete: false` | the sources of each window kept with the aggregate, at a memory cost that was not measured |
| 2b | The canvas beyond its scripted checks (Ch. 12, 12.4) | The canvas at the canvas of evaluation 11 reads the references and the meter declarations of protocol 3.12 and has a `Lift` card (from set A's canvas checks). | 2c | Resume against real Event Hubs (Ch. 10, Propositions 10.7 and 10.8) | No Azure deployment exists, and the AKS cluster the captures came from no longer exists (its resource group was deleted on 2026-11-10, not by this project; reported in set A), so no `azd up`, no further real capture and no run against a hub is possible until a cluster exists. Resumption and the order receive, journal, record, process are tested against a fake source, and the Event Hubs source is code that no test and no run reads from; it reads one partition | a cluster, `azd up`, a live run with a forced kill and a restart from the store |
| 2c | A restart after a kill during ingestion (Ch. 13, H10) | The benchmark restarts after a clean stop. `LiveResumeTests` and `LiveCrashTests` simulate a kill at each step of an envelope with an exception, on the in-memory store, which is not a process kill and not a cut in the middle of a journal entry | the B10 of Ch. 13: kill the process during B1, restart, compare the digest |

The first capture spans 5,679 s but its flows cover 725 s, and a restart of its journal (6 to 7 s) is a data point on real data, not on an hour of steady input. | 2c | Backfilled input on a live source (Ch. 7, D31) | A live source keeps a late horizon of three window widths to bound its state. Input with old timestamps (an Activity Log backfill, a Prometheus range query) is dropped as late and can change derived states. Not listed here, because they are listed where they arise: `Union` conflict policies other than `preserveEvidence` (Ch. 5), document truncation (Ch. 12, O12.7), the snapshot seal of Def. 11.8 in object storage, change-feed followers and hub buckets (Ch. 11), ADX (Ch. 7, 11), evidence history and provenance semirings (Ch. 9), split of identities (Ch. 1), and the open list of 14.10.

**Evidence.**
- An engine with 19 of the 20 operator kinds, in-memory, SQLite and Cosmos (emulator) stores with a journal, restart from the store and a state seal, one lens, identity merge with an order-independent survivor, a configuration edit by full replay, refactoring, evidence elements retained for 24 hours of logical time, document projections and a live input exists; the Cosmos store has not run against a real account (Ch. 14). Theorems are paper proofs, not mechanized ones. Property tests cover Ch. 1, 3 and 4 (4,000 seeded cases per property) and the lens (generated edit sequences on one lens). Theorems about unbuilt parts (T12, provenance semirings, the carry policy) have no engine evidence, and T9 and T10 have acyclic example evidence only. The live input's resumption and crash order are tested against a fake source only. A restart from a state seal does not recompute the journal before the seal, by design (Ch. 11, Proposition 11.10). The premises B1 and B2 of Theorem 6.2 have unit tests (Ch. 14, 14.5). The real captures are local, the cluster they came from no longer exists, and only the fixtures and the synthetic `estate` can be replayed from the repository.
- Performance figures are one run each (ten for H5, H7, H8 and H10, on a machine under load; none was on a machine with no other load) on one laptop-class machine with synthetic workloads for scale and a small real capture (Ch. 14, 14.7). H5 (engine part, memory and SQLite), H8 (hot plane, head revision) and H10 (restart from SQLite, with and without a state seal) are `supported` for those configurations only, H7 has two different labels on the Cosmos emulator from two sets of runs and is open for a real account, and none says anything about the reference hardware. At evaluation 6 the benchmarks of H5 and H10 run with an almost empty health model (Ch. 13, 13.9). H9 is met in one run by the second hot-graph layout (337 MB), and the window close at 240,000 pairs is above the latency target of H4 and H12 in one run. The Cosmos and ADX figures of Ch. 11 are assumptions.
- Theorem 6.1 (T5) is proved relative to two theorems of [budiu2023] that it states and does not reprove. Theorems 8.3 and 9.7 are proved in their chapters without a cited premise. Nine statements are proved under named premises on programs or on an external system (inventory), and a premise is not evidence until its test exists.

**Language and semantics** (gate 1b).
- The kernel is closed. A new operator, builtin function, value kind or adapter needs an engine release (Ch. 2, 5, 10). Ontologies, identity domains and projections do not.
- Recursion is positive, monotone and over a finite domain (Theorem 6.5, condition F5). General recursion, negation inside recursion, fresh identities in recursion and well-founded semantics are out of scope. `Join` is not a lens step in v1. `sync` has no negative application conditions.
- Efficient maintenance of recursive views under retraction is cited, not established (remark after Theorem 6.6). Multi-hop patterns are not constant-time maintainable in general (Fact 11.2).
- Histograms and distributions as a telemetry kind are open (Q15 of [R03]). `mean` is not decomposable and must be rolled up through a weighted mean.
- Virtual graphs and symbolic patches (`DiffProfile.Symbolic`) are not modelled (Ch. 0).

**Operation** (gate 1c and Phase 2).
- One writer per graph. Multi-writer masters and commutative merge are out of scope (Ch. 4).
- Backward edits change the internal graph only. No write-back to Azure or Kubernetes (Theorem 8.6).
- Evidence is bounded: edits are not recomputation from scratch for row-input projections once retention is bounded (Theorem 9.2); the engine retains the whole input log and replays all of it (Ch. 9, 9.5). Aligned frontiers are open (Def. 6.6).
- A graph space must fit one node's hot graph (Ch. 11, R15). Valid-time (observed-time) indexing of the structural plane is not provided.
- Entity merge is implemented as additive records with a smaller-id survivor and tested by examples (Theorem 1.7, [R03] section 5); split is not. Stability under concurrent projections and generated arrival orders is open (Q10).

- Refactoring preservation (Proposition 5.7) is proved under six premises, of which the engine checks two and reports `preserves: union` regardless; the fixture tests are on one module. The health model reaches along `Affects` to depth 16 and gives no diagnostic for a longer chain (Proposition 7.8). Replay is batch invariant on both real captures at five chunk sizes since identity merge and the two fixes of D17; the premise about identity order has no generated test.
- The engine consumes five of the forwarder's markers, on fixture tests only. A `dropped` marker marks a telemetry window and does not mark the graph stale (D19). Evidence elements grow with the event rate times the retention age (24 hours in the definitions, a choice and not a result); at the audit rate of the second capture that would be about 3.6 M elements, which is arithmetic and not a measurement (Ch. 9, 9.6).

**Assumptions the paper leans on.** Collision resistance of SHA-256 (Ch. 1), the store properties S1 to S5 (Ch. 11), the closedness of the language (Proposition 5.4), recorded input order as the determinism boundary (Theorem 6.3), and coverage declarations being true (Theorem 10.3).
