## 12.1 Consumers of graph feeds

A graph has no outputs of its own. Everything a person sees is produced by a **subscriber** of graph feeds: the canvas draws graphs, a document projection renders blocks, an external system mirrors state. All of them use the one mechanism of Ch. 3 and Ch. 11: a sealed snapshot followed by adjacent revisions.

**Definition 12.1 (graph feed).** The feed of graph `g` is the pair of a snapshot `Snap(r_s)` with its seal and the stream of commit records `r_s + 1, r_s + 2, …` from `Follow` (Def. 11.3), plus, separately, the telemetry feed of its plane (closed windows and series reads, Def. 11.4). A feed has no gaps (Theorem 11.5), or it reports `Gap`.

**Definition 12.2 (replica).** A replica of graph `g` is a consumer-side copy `R` initialised from a snapshot at `r_s` and updated by applying each received patch with the guard of Def. 3.7. A patch whose revision is not the successor of the replica's, or whose before state does not match, is **rejected and reported**, never applied silently and never dropped without a counter.

**Definition 12.3 (the seam).** The canvas consumes graphs through `GraphSource`: `open()` returns the graph space (each graph with role, descriptor, ontology, producing definition and inputs), `snapshot(graph)` returns the initial state, `subscribe(graph, sink)` delivers `patch`, `rejected` and `end` events in order, `telemetry` is an optional feed, `correspondences(graph)` optionally returns derived-to-source element records, `evaluate(definition)` returns `unavailable` or an output snapshot. Wire records are the schemas of `brainstorm/contracts/schemas/`, validated with the schema files themselves.

## 12.2 The canvas as a subscriber

**Theorem 12.1 (replica correctness).** A replica that follows Definition 12.2 over a feed holds `G_r` after applying the patch of revision `r`, for every `r`, and holds a state equal to the engine's at that revision. A scoped replica (Def. 3.12) holds the scoped state.
Status: **proved** from Theorems 11.5 and 11.7 and Def. 3.7, for a replica that implements Def. 12.2; the proof below is complete for the model and was read only. That the canvas implements Def. 12.2 is tested, with test evidence on the canvas side for the application and rejection rules (against a fake socket) and engine side for order, resume, gap and ahead-of-head. The end-to-end statement (engine output replayed by the canvas, replica digest equal to the engine digest at each revision) is an **obligation** (O12.1).

*Proof.* The replica starts at `G_{r_s}` (the snapshot is the state at its revision, Theorem 11.7). Each accepted patch has the guard of Def. 3.7, so applying it gives the next state by Def. 3.8. By Theorem 11.5 the received patches are the log in order, so no revision is skipped. A rejected patch stops the replica from advancing and is visible, so a replica is never silently ahead of or behind the log. ∎

**Proposition 12.2 (late join).** A client that joins late reads history over the snapshot and patch reads, and follows live over a stream. The two parts agree if the follow starts at the revision after the last read patch.
Status: **proved** (Theorem 6.4). Protocol version 3 realizes it (12.5): one subscription yields a snapshot per graph with its sequence number and then every later event, and `after` resumes.

**Theorem 12.3 (same entity across graphs).** Two elements in different graphs of a space with the same entity id denote the same entity, if both ids were produced through one identity domain and equal canonical components. An aggregate output has a new identity and never reuses a member's id. The canvas highlighting of "the same entity id in every graph" is therefore sound for entities and says nothing about aggregates.
Status: the first sentence is **proved** (Theorem 1.4: one key gives one id). Its converse, that one entity has one id, holds only through `Rep`: after a merge a graph that was not evaluated again can still hold the element under the id that did not survive, and the highlight then misses it without being wrong. The second sentence is a rule for authors (ADR-094, Def. 5.9) and not a consequence of typing: this paper found no judgment in Ch. 5 (5.2) that refuses an emit whose identity is a member's id carried through an aggregate. Both remarks were added at the second reading of gate 1a.

## 12.3 Telemetry in a viewer

The canvas holds telemetry in a ring of quantized frames per metric (8 bits per value, 61 ticks) and draws it as GPU channels (`canvas/README.md`, "Telemetry"). The paper's telemetry plane (Ch. 7) is keyed by `(g, k, p, τ)` and has no revisions, so a single slider that moves "revision and telemetry together" needs a defined alignment.

**Definition 12.4 (time alignment).** `rev_of(τ)` is the largest revision of graph `g` whose logical time is at most `τ`, read from the commit records (Ch. 11, 11.8). The aligned element view at slider position `τ` is `view(k, rev_of(τ), [τ − w, τ))` (Def. 7.9).

**Theorem 12.4 (alignment is well defined).** `rev_of` is monotone and defined for every `τ` at or after the first commit, and the aligned view is a function of `(G, M, τ)`.
Status: **proved**.

*Proof.* The *logical time of a revision* is the largest `λ` input among the inputs up to the cause of its commit. The commit record of Def. 3.8 does not hold it, so it is read from the input log, or from a field that the store adds (Ch. 11, 11.8). It is non-decreasing in the revision: causes follow the recorded order (F1 of Theorem 3.5), and `λ` is monotone per projection (Ch. 3, 3.4). The first form of this proof cited Def. 3.8 and Theorem 3.5, neither of which mentions logical time. The largest revision with `λ ≤ τ` therefore exists, and larger `τ` gives a revision at least as large. The view is a function of its arguments (Theorem 7.6). ∎

The prototype does not have `rev_of`. The engine's revisions carry no commit time, so the canvas keeps one time axis for the whole space, puts telemetry frames at their window start and places revisions by order on the telemetry windows, and the timeline says so. The correct source of the map is `rev_of`, which the engine does not export (O12.5).

## 12.4 Definition graphs, editing and presentation in the canvas

**Proposition 12.5 (definitions need no new mechanism).** Definitions are graphs of the catalog (Def. 2.1). A client can therefore subscribe to the catalog graph and to the definition modules exactly as to a data graph, and Theorem 12.1 applies. A change of a definition is a configuration edit (Ch. 9), proposed as a patch on `catalog` (on a draft, Ch. 4, 4.6).
Status: **proved** (Def. 2.1, Theorem 12.1). **Implemented in the prototype.** The engine serves `catalog` like any data graph with the kernel as its ontology (`GET /graphs/catalog/snapshot`, `patches`, `ontology`), with definition ids as element ids and relationship ids `<source id>|<edge type>|<target id>`. The canvas reads it (modules, types, identity domains, projections, operators, ports, wires, rules with direction class) and its editor edits it through drafts. In the canvas measurement of `canvas/PERF.md` the `catalog` graph had 885 nodes. The engine README reports 1,048 entities and 1,969 relationships for the served catalog graph together with the kernel ontology, validated against the canvas schemas; the two counts are of different engine versions. The canvas also keeps a checker and editor model for the `ProjectionDefinition` of the contracts (`canvas/src/core/projection/*`), tested and not reachable from the UI.
The projection editor shows what the checker accepts: its palette comes from `GET /palette`, derived from the kernel table and the engine's support list, and operators the engine cannot evaluate are listed disabled with the reason. Wiring is type checked in the client (`commands.canWire`) and again by the engine on every patch, with diagnostics by definition element id.

**The shell and its state** (`canvas/README.md`). Fluent v9 components and tokens everywhere, with the themes `webLightTheme`, `webDarkTheme` and `teamsHighContrastTheme`. Custom drawing is limited to the WebGL view and the editor's SVG node canvas. One Zustand store holds plain, serializable state (summaries and UI state, never graphs) with Redux-style actions and a pure reducer. The mutable side (workspace, histories, GL panes, telemetry stores, the open draft) lives outside the store. `dispatch` runs the effects in order and then the reducer, and every server push becomes an action (`feed/patch`, `feed/telemetry`, `feed/telemetryReset`, `feed/resync`, `feed/connection`, `feed/rejected`). Patches and telemetry frames do not change the state themselves: effects report throttled summaries (at most every 100 ms for data, 250 ms for time), so frames never render React components. This is the reducer contract of Proposition 12.9 realized in the client.

**Resume and caching.** The client reads the sequence number before the history, subscribes with `after = seq`, and resumes after the last sequence number it saw when the connection returns. Duplicates are dropped by revision (patches) and window time (telemetry). The shell shows "stale" while disconnected and "resynchronizing" after a gap. History is read from revision-addressed URLs, which are revalidated by a content tag (Proposition 12.10) and answer `304` when nothing changed, so the canvas needs no IndexedDB. The canvas README's measurement of a reload served from the browser cache without a request (`transferSize` 0) was taken when those URLs were marked immutable; it does not describe the current engine. The canvas README records runs against `sattle serve` on the lab fixtures and on a real capture (first view in 100 to 160 ms, live arrival on a paced replay, resume after killing the engine), which this paper did not repeat.

**Accessibility and its limits.** The Elements tab shows the same graph as a Fluent DataGrid (nodes or edges, filterable, selection synced both ways) and the inspector lists connections as a Tree that navigates with Enter. This is the keyboard and screen reader alternative to the WebGL canvas, and the only view when WebGL2 is missing. Tests: axe on every view, the three themes and both dialogs in jsdom (structure, names, roles), keyboard tests of the element list, the connections tree and the shortcut dialog, and a script in Chrome for what jsdom cannot do (color contrast on every view in light, dark and high contrast, tab order, arrow keys on toolbar and tab list, dialog focus in and back to the opener, visible focus, the editor keyboard-only). Result of this paper's run at the canvas of evaluation 8 on the `estate` fixture (`paper/evidence/a11y-head8.txt`): 0 axe violations in all 32 page and dialog checks, all 12 keyboard and highlight checks pass, and every role color of the legend is present in the three themes. The script found, and the canvas fixed, button and card names that did not contain their visible text and a dialog that did not return focus. **Limits reported:** no screen reader pass (NVDA, VoiceOver); the WebGL canvas is one focusable group whose content is reached through the Elements list and the inspector; dragging in the editor has no pointer-free equivalent beyond Shift+arrows (move), `W` (connect) and `M` (region select); Chrome only.

**Value roles: how a viewer maps health to color.** The engine serves roles as ontology data (Ch. 2, Def. 2.13); the viewer decides what they look like.
- When a graph's ontology has roles for a property of nodes (or of edges), that property colors them by default, with no user action. Another property, or type colors, can be chosen per graph and separately for nodes and edges, each with its own control and legend. The override is not stored on the engine.
- The roles map to Fluent status tokens (the success, warning and danger foregrounds; the neutral foregrounds for `neutral` and `unknown`) of the light or dark theme for the canvas and of the active theme for the legend. A value without a role is neutral.
- Color is never the only cue. The legend lists value, count and the role with its glyph and word (`✓ ⚠ ✖ ?`, `○` for neutral). Labels carry the value and the glyph, danger edges are dashed, and the Elements list has a column with the value and the role word. In high contrast the glyph and word carry the role.
- Colors follow patches and the timeline: a patch that changes the property recolors the element, and moving the time slider shows the state at that revision, legend counts included.
- The canvas reads the `presentation` entries of the ontology and removes them before schema validation, because the contract schema `OntologyDefinition.schema.json` forbids extra fields on property definitions (the schema needs a `presentation` entry, which the engine README asks for).
- Roles are edited in the Definitions tab ("Value roles") as `ValueRole` nodes under the property.
- When a target has roles for several properties (the `health` graph: `Component.state`, `Signal.degraded`, `Signal.unhealthy`) the default keeps all of them: an element takes the worst role among the hinted properties it has (danger, warning, unknown, ok, neutral), its text names the property (`state: Healthy`), and the legend and the element list say so. The accessibility script checks this per theme on the `estate` engine: with no coloring chosen, a screenshot of the canvas must contain pixels of every role color of the legend, every element of a type with a hinted property must have a role, and the legend names each role.
- Reported by the canvas README and not repeated here: on the `deploy-health` fixture, nodes and `Talks` edges are colored by `acme.deployhealth.state` with no user action, the legend lists `✓ Healthy (2)` and, at the unhealthy revisions, `✖ Unhealthy`, and the timeline of 10 points goes Healthy, both Unhealthy, one of each, Unhealthy again and back to Healthy.

## 12.5 Protocol version 3 as the feed, and the mismatches of the first export

**Definition 12.7 (ordered event stream).** The server owns the committed state and emits one stream of events, each with a server-wide sequence number `seq` that increases by one (`snapshot`, `patch`, `telemetry`, `activation`, `draft`, `end`, `error`; `time` is unsequenced). A subscription names graphs and optionally `after = seq`. Without `after` it receives one snapshot per graph, current as of its `seq`, and then every later event. With `after` it receives the later events, or, when `after` is older than the retained events (the last 20,000 by default, `serve --retain-events`, D30; the patches of a graph are kept for the last 2,000 revisions by default, `--retain-patches`, and an older revision answers `410 Gap`), a `gap` and then snapshots. A client ahead of the server gets `AheadOfHead`. A subscription that has more than 10,000 queued messages is closed with `SlowConsumer`.

**Proposition 12.9 (reducer contract).** A client that applies a `patch` of graph `g` iff `patch.from` equals its local head of `g`, and otherwise resubscribes with `after` equal to the last `seq` it applied, holds for every graph a state equal to the engine's state at some revision, and never skips or repeats a revision.
Status: **proved** from Theorem 12.1: a client that applies a patch only at its own head is a replica in the sense of Def. 12.2 per graph, and a resubscription with `after` restarts the feed at the next event, which Theorem 11.5 makes gap-free or reports as a `gap`. Read only. Tested: the stream's order, resume, gap and ahead-of-head on the engine; on the client, reconnect, resume after the last sequence number and duplicate dropping by revision (patches) and window time (telemetry) against a fake socket, and one live exercise (killing and restarting the engine), recorded in `canvas/README.md`. The `gap`, `AheadOfHead`, `SlowConsumer` and `activation` messages are tested against a fake socket only.

**Proposition 12.10 (resources, tags and caching).** (a) Within one run of one store, a response addressed by a revision (`GET /graphs/{g}/snapshot?revision=r`, `patches?after=a&to=b` with `b` not above the head, `document?revision=r`) is a function of its URL, and a response addressed by a digest (`GET /releases/{digest}`, its `text` and `diff`) is a function of the digest. (b) A revision number, a catalog revision and a sequence number are unique only within one run of one store: two runs with different definitions reach the same catalog revision, and two runs can have the same graph revision with different content. Therefore only resources whose URL contains a content digest are `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`; every other cached resource is `no-cache` and is revalidated; and **every** `ETag` is the first 32 hexadecimal digits of SHA-256 over the media type, a newline and the body bytes, nothing else going into it. Equal bytes have equal tags in any run and after a restart from a store, different bytes never share a tag, `If-None-Match` with a tag the server does not know answers `200` with the body and the current tag, and a matching tag answers `304`.
Status: (a) **proved** from the immutability of history (Theorem 3.2) and the content addressing of releases (Theorem 2.4); (b) is a **definition of the engine's behavior** with a **tested** consequence (tested: two hosts whose definitions differ in one value role answer different tags for the ontology at the same catalog revision; the same content has the same tag after a restart from a store and in another host instance; a stale `If-None-Match` answers the body and a matching one answers `304` without building the body while nothing changed; the tag depends on the media type and on nothing but the content). **D29 (found in set A, fixed).** Tags used to be built from a graph name and a revision (`"onto-health-16"`), and revision-addressed responses were marked immutable, which is true within one run only. After a rebuild with changed definitions a browser revalidated the ontology with its old tag, was told `304` and kept an ontology without value roles. Part (b) is the rule that replaced it (protocol 3.9).

The first engine export differed from the canvas contract in eight points (D16). Each is listed with the resolution this paper gives and its state in the prototype:

## 12.6 Documents as projections

A document projection is a configured transformation from graph facts to an ordered list of structured blocks. The engine realizes it **without a kernel change**: a document is a derived graph whose output ontology is `sattle.doc`, and any projection can produce one (protocol 3.4, `engine/definitions/sattle.doc.sattle`).

**Definition 12.5 (document, block, patch).** A document is `D = (order, blocks)` with `order` a list of block ids without repetition and `blocks` a map from block id to block content (heading, paragraph, table with keyed rows, list, code). A document has its own integer revision, a vector of source graph revisions, the projection version and a completeness flag `Complete` or `ExplicitlyTruncated`. A document patch is `P = (E, order_before, order_after, sources_before, sources_after)` with `E` a set of block edits `(id, before, after)` with distinct ids. Orders and edits are independent.
**Block identity.** The id of a block is derived from the rule id and the source identity or group key, never from the row position or a label: `bid = base32(H(JCS([rule, group key])))`. A table row has a row key from the declared row identity fields. Row order must be total, so templates append a stable identity tie-breaker (`ARCHITECTURE.md`).

**Definition 12.8 (document graph, engine realization).** The ontology `sattle.doc` has one entity type `Block { kind, order, text?, level?, a?, b?, c?, d?, n? }` and the identity domain `block(rule, key)`, so the block id is derived from the rule and the source key. `kind` is `heading` (`level` 1 to 6), `paragraph`, `item`, `stat` (`text` is the label, `n` the value), `header` or `row` (cells `a` to `d`, or `n` instead of `d`); consecutive `header` and `row` blocks form one table. `order` is a text sort key, and blocks are ordered by `order` (ordinal) and then by id, so the order is total. `GET /graphs/{g}/document[?revision=r][&format=markdown|json]` renders the graph at the head or at a revision, and the graph entry in `GET /space` carries `document: true`. A graph that is not a document answers `404 NotADocument`.

**Differences from Def. 12.5 (D24).** The document is the graph, so its revision is the graph's revision, a document patch is a graph patch (inversion is Theorem 4.3 and not the separate statement of Theorem 12.7), and `order` is a property of each block and not a list independent of the blocks. The source vector of Def. 12.5 is not part of the rendered document. The completeness flag and `ExplicitlyTruncated` are not built, so a document over an unbounded graph has no declared truncation.

**Theorem 12.6 (stable block identity, T14).** Block ids are invariant under reordering of the output, under change of a label and under change of row position. Two blocks have the same id iff the rule id and the group key are equal, unless `H` has a collision.
Status: **proved** (Theorem 1.1: `bid` is a function of canonical bytes of `[rule, key]` and of nothing else). The engine's identity domain `block(rule, key)` has exactly these components.

**Theorem 12.7 (document patch inversion, T14).** If `apply(P, D)` is defined then `apply(inverse(P), apply(P, D)) = D`, where `inverse(P)` swaps before and after in every edit, in the orders and in the source vectors, and swaps the revisions. A patch applied to a document at a different revision, order or content is rejected.
Status: **proved** on the model of Def. 12.5. The engine realizes documents as graphs, where the statement is Theorem 4.3 for graph patches.

*Proof.* Edits are keyed by distinct block ids and orders are separate lists, so `apply` is the same as the guarded edit of Def. 3.7 on the map `blocks` and a replacement of the list `order`, whose guards are the exact base. The inverse has the matching guards on the result, so the argument of Theorem 4.3 applies to each of the two components. A different base fails a guard. ∎

**Theorem 12.8 (rendering is a function, escaping).** A renderer maps a document snapshot to bytes and is a function of the snapshot and the pinned renderer version. Text from graph data is escaped, so it cannot create document structure.
Status: **function: proved by definition, and tested. Escaping: tested by four examples, not a property** (O12.3). The renderer writes LF line endings and one final newline, escapes a backslash before ``\ ` * _ { } [ ] < > ( ) # + - . ! | ~`` in data text, and turns a line break into a space. Tests: four escape cases (plain text, a table pipe, markdown heading and link syntax, a line break); blocks are rendered in the order given by `order` and then id, and the order in which the graph was built does not matter; a document is the same bytes at every revision in two replays of the same input. The contract oracle's single hostile paragraph is not a property either.

**Proposition 12.11 (a document digest per revision).** The digest of a document at revision `r` is `sha256` of the rendered Markdown bytes. Equal graph states give equal documents and equal digests, and the digest changes when a rendered block changes. `X-Document-Digest` carries it, the ETag is the content tag of Proposition 12.10 (a digest of the body and its media type, so the tag differs between formats and is equal after a restart), and both `GET ...?revision=r` and the head response are `no-cache` and revalidated, the head response with the `Content-Location` of the revision URL.
Status: **proved** from Theorem 12.8 (function), **tested by example and measured**. On the fixture `deploy-health` (a synthetic shop, `paper/evidence/document-head.txt`) the report has two rows at the head, and earlier revisions show the Deployment `db` `Unhealthy` with 0 ready pods at revision 5, `web` with 2 ready pods at revision 3, and an empty table at revision 1; the digests differ per revision (`DocumentTests`: the document changes when a Deployment turns `Unhealthy`). On the first real capture the document of the report (`deploymentReport`, 26 blocks: 1 heading, 2 stat, 1 header, 22 row, 29 lines) has the same digest `sha256:7d2b8be6…f2690` at chunk size 1, where the graph is at revision 60, and at chunk size 100,000, where it is at revision 39. The document is a function of the graph state and not of the revision numbers. The text of that document is not kept because it names workloads of the lab cluster.

**Worked example** (`engine/definitions/acme.report.sattle`, graph `deploymentReport` over `deploymentHealth`). A heading, two `stat` blocks (deployments and unhealthy deployments, by `Group`), a table header, and one `row` block per Deployment keyed by cluster, namespace and name. The projection is ordinary definition text: `Values` for the title and the header, `Group` for the totals, `EmitEntity` of `doc.Block` with `identity doc.block(rule, key)`. Nothing in the engine knows the report; the engine knows the ontology `sattle.doc` and one renderer.

**Remarks and gaps.**
- The kernel needs no document sink: the design amendment O12.2 is resolved by an ontology and one generic renderer with fixed block kinds. A renderer for another block kind or another format (JSON is the second) is an engine release.
- A document over an unbounded graph needs a finite selection or an explicit truncation (`ExplicitlyTruncated`). The engine has neither, so a large graph renders in full.
- Markdown byte editing and write-back from documents to graphs are not implied. A document patch is a patch of the graph.
- Documents follow graph feeds, so a document is itself a derived, versioned output with its own log, and Theorem 11.5 applies to its subscribers.
- **Document view in the canvas**. A graph whose entry in `/space` has `"document": true` shows in the data view as a page with the tabs Document and Graph. The page is the Markdown of `GET /graphs/{g}/document?revision=N` for the revision the timeline shows, so scrubbing changes the document, and at the head it follows the event stream (the graph's version changes and the view asks again with `If-None-Match`, keeping its copy on `304`). The canvas reads the fixed Markdown subset the engine writes (headings, lists, tables, escaped data text) and draws it with Fluent `Title`, `Text` and `Table`.
  - *Tested* (unit and component tests, `canvas-tests-head8.txt`): the example report parses to blocks, the graph entry says whether it is a document, a revision is fetched once and then served from the cache, the head revalidates with its tag and keeps its copy on `304`, the view follows the timeline and a patch, an empty revision says so, a failing engine gives a message, and axe finds no violation in jsdom. In Chrome the accessibility script finds 0 violations on the document view in three themes (`a11y-head8.txt`).
  - *Follows the live stream* (`npm run document-live`, `document-live-head8.txt`): against an engine that plays `lab/fixtures` at `--speed 2`, with the page opened once and never reloaded, `deploymentReport` passes through revisions 2 and 3 and ends at revision 3 with the digest that `GET /graphs/{g}/document` gives (revisions 1, 2 and 3 in the run of evaluation 6, `document-live-head6.txt`; which revisions the page sees depends on when it opens).
  - *Not tested:* the view shows the first 19 characters of the revision's digest in a badge; whether it equals the digest the engine reports at that revision is not compared (O12.1).

## 12.7 Delta to iteration 0.1

- **Canvas contract.** The `GraphSource` seam is the consumer contract. Its `ProjectionDefinition` is replaced by catalog graph subscription (Proposition 12.5), which the prototype implements.
- **Protocol version 3.** The ordered event stream with resume, revision-addressed resources revalidated by content tags, drafts, releases, preview and refactoring are not in the contracts. Def. 12.7 and Propositions 12.9 and 12.10 record them.
- **Telemetry in the viewer.** Alignment by `rev_of` (Def. 12.4) replaces "tick k is revision k".
- **Documents.** Block ids by rule and key. In the engine the order is a property of the block and the document is a graph of the ontology `sattle.doc` (Def. 12.8); no kernel sink was needed (O12.2 resolved).
- **Findings 32 to 34.** The oracle's document tests are finite examples. T14 is now proved on the model and still tested only by the finite oracle.
