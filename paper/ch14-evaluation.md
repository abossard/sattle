**Decision rule.** Chapter 13 (13.6) fixes the rule for hypotheses: a latency or size target is *supported* iff the statistic is at or below the target in every one of at least 10 independent runs, *refuted* iff the median run is above it, otherwise *inconclusive*. A status is stated for a configuration (store, machine, load): see 13.6 for how the rule is applied to a machine that is not the reference of Def. 13.4. A figure from fewer than ten runs is reported as *met in one run*, *not met in one run* or *not measured*. H5 (engine part, memory and SQLite), H8 (hot plane at the head revision) and H10 (restart from SQLite, with and without a state seal, at 1.2 h and 4.1 h of repeated capture) are `supported` in that sense. H7 has two labels on the Cosmos emulator from two sets of runs (`refuted` and `inconclusive`) and no status for a real account. Every other hypothesis is open (14.7).

## 14.2 The lab

- **Cluster.** An AKS cluster with Azure CNI powered by Cilium (it no longer exists: its resource group was deleted on 2026-11-10, not by this project, as the orchestrator reports; the two captures were taken before that), ACNS observability enabled for Hubble relay, Kubernetes 1.34, managed Prometheus (`lab/README.md`, `lab/CAPTURE-FORMAT.md`).
- **Workload.** Namespace `sattle-demo` with `frontend`, `api`, `db`, `loadgen` and an `intruder` that network policy denies (`lab/README.md`).
- **Capture tooling.** `lab/capture/capture.sh` records, per source, NDJSON envelopes (`v`, `source`, `capturedAt`, `seq`, `key`, `meta`, `payload`). Sources captured: Azure Resource Graph, Kubernetes list and watch, Hubble flows, Prometheus range queries, Azure Monitor metrics, Activity Log backfill, VNet flow logs and Event Hubs mirrors (`lab/CAPTURE-FORMAT.md`). A forwarder (`lab/capture/eh-forward.mjs`) publishes the same envelopes to an Event Hub for the live input (Ch. 10, 10.6).
- **Deployment.** The lab is an azd project rooted in `lab/` with hooks (Ch. 10, 10.5). The lab session measured one real `azd up` (3 min 14 s) and one `azd down` (2 min 23 s) on the existing cluster, and the state after teardown against a baseline taken before (`lab/README.md`; not re-run by this paper, which cannot deploy to Azure). What teardown leaves behind: subscription deployment records, a soft-deleted storage account, ACNS leftovers in `kube-system`, and `advancedNetworking` reading `enabled: false` where it read `null`. A second `azd up` was not run.
- **Captures used.** Two. `20261110T105738Z` (`paper/evidence/replay-head6-105738.txt`): 245,345 records (243,979 in evaluation 1, before the Event Hubs diagnostic records were read), 235,858 Hubble flows over 725 s plus 522 VNet flow log records with 175,265 tuples, a capture span of 5,679 s. `20261110T140542Z` (`replay-head6-140542.txt`): 160,300 records, 146,759 Hubble records, 1,148 VNet flow log records, records from every source, a capture span of 1,017 s. No further capture can be taken while no cluster exists. Both captures are gitignored and stay local. The tracked space file binds the health model to invented names; the real workload arguments are in a gitignored overlay next to the captures (`lab/captures/space-overlay.sattle`), which `replay` and `serve` apply to a capture under `lab/captures`. This paper's runs used a copy of that overlay that matches the committed definitions.
- **VNet flow logs.** Tenant policy blocks public network access to the storage account. They are read through a short-lived pod in the cluster (`lab/README.md`, "Flow log blobs"). No `lost_events` records occurred in this capture.
- **Fixtures.** `lab/fixtures/` holds scrubbed samples with the same envelope (`lab/fixtures/build.sh`). The engine tests replay them (`ReplayTests.cs`). They were rebuilt once from the first capture with a stricter scrubber, so their state digest changed (the orchestrator reports `a90c5be7…` before and `43917169…` after; this paper cites no fixture digest elsewhere). The tracked space file binds these invented names, so the fixture is served with `sattle serve DIR --cluster synth` and no overlay. It is also the default source of the canvas (Ch. 12, 12.4).
- **Size.** The lab estate is small. After replay of the first capture the master graph has 15,819 entities and 5,537 relationships, of which 15,363 entities are evidence elements, kept for 24 h of logical time (Ch. 9, 9.6; without the retention rule 16,777 entities, 16,321 of them evidence elements); the other 456 entities are the estate itself. ## 14.9 Threats to validity

**Construct.**
- The hypotheses of Ch. 13 name Azure hardware and Cosmos. The measurements are on a laptop-class machine with in-memory and SQLite stores and, for the Cosmos store, the vNext emulator, which reports a request charge of 1 and is not a latency model of a real account. A pass here is not a pass there, and `H3`, `H7`, `H13` cannot be confirmed without a real Cosmos account.
- H4 and H12 were measured with one `Advance` call that includes two graphs. The hypothesis concerns a per-hop latency over a stack of at least three projections.
- The synthetic benchmark workload has 5,000 pair series (`engine/PERF.md`). The reference estate has about 240,000 flow pairs.

**Internal.**
- Mostly one run per figure, on a machine that was shared with other work sessions in the case of the canvas. `engine/PERF.md` reports no variance for the single-run figures; the ten-run figures (H5, H7, H8, H10) report median, minimum, maximum and an order-statistic interval. **A machine with no other load was not available to the orchestrator session, the engine session or this paper**: the orchestrator's ten-run figures were taken at a load average of 25 to 106 at `588a256` and 25 to 75 at `9d0fff1` (recorded per run in `perf-*-orch*.txt`), the engine session's at 14 to 115, and the runs of this paper at 27 to 91 at `588a256`, 16 to 70 at `9d0fff1` (one set of three restarts at 100 to 239) and 16 to 82 at `6f784c9`. The label a figure gets is therefore scoped to that load (Ch. 13, 13.6). For an upper-bound target, load can only raise latencies, so a pass under load holds a fortiori and a failure under load is weak evidence; the one failure, H7 on the emulator, is also on a store that is not the target. The estate run is one run of four windows.
- The property tests are by the same authors as the proofs. The generators are seeded and the properties test the engine types, not an independent implementation. Only the kernel has mutation tests.
- Several theorem statements were checked on fixtures and on one real capture, not on randomly generated projections (T5, 6.1; 5.2).
- Wall times of replays and test runs are single runs on a laptop that other sessions were using, so they are not performance figures (default engine run of 456 tests 1 m 52 s at `64f7a6a`; 403 tests in 2 m 6 s, 96 slow tests in 5 m 4 s and 18 capture tests in 3 m 52 s at `6f784c9`, at load 45 to 90). Evaluation 1 replayed with a Debug build, every later one with a Release build. The capture-category tests need the two real captures linked into the export; in evaluations 1 to 3 they were not, and the tests that use a capture returned early.
- The investigation of D21 compared exported snapshots and telemetry with `jq` and did not instrument the engine, so its first cause was a reading of counters. That the merge removes the mismatch is a measurement on two inputs, and that the order-independent survivor removes the dependence in general is a sketch (Proposition 1.8) with one synthetic test and two captures. The causes of D17 were found by the engine session, not by this paper. The unit tests of B1 and B2 were absent from the suite in evaluations 4 and 5; a regression of the D17 causes was caught only by the capture tests in that time, and only on the shapes in those captures.
- From evaluation 10 on the paper's numbers at the current commit are the orchestrator's and not this paper's: the same session that directs the work verifies it, and this paper's check is a reading of code and tests. The evidence files in `paper/evidence/` end at evaluation 9.
- Canvas and engine were evaluated at different times. The canvas tests, the walkthroughs on the `estate` and the accessibility script are at canvas `53db46e` against engines `64f7a6a` and `4841a36`. The canvas at `14c2965`, with its `Lift` card, its regenerated default source and the references route, was checked by the orchestrator and not by this paper. The walkthrough row on the newest real capture is at `478e2f3` against `6f784c9` and was not repeated. What the canvas shows on the served newest capture (589 stubs, 143 collapsed evidence elements, the propagated failure) was seen in a browser by the orchestrator and is not asserted by a script.
- Every evaluation is of a commit, not of the working tree, which held uncommitted changes of other sessions. The real-capture runs depend on a gitignored overlay file that only this machine has and that other sessions edit; this paper used a copy that matches the committed definitions (the state digests confirm the copy), after one batch of runs in evaluation 6 failed to load with the shared file and was repeated.
- The lab figures (azd times, state after teardown) are the lab session's own measurements. This paper did not deploy to Azure and cannot repeat them, and nobody can at present: the cluster no longer exists.
- **Disagreements between the repository's documents and its code** as last checked by this paper (engine documents at `4841a36`, canvas README at `14c2965`). (1) `engine/PERF.md` gives 578 tests for the full run without the emulator; this paper counts 579 as the sum of its three runs (463, 96 and 20), which agrees with the orchestrator's 598 with the emulator less the 19 Cosmos cases. The default count, 463, agrees. (2) A comment in `canvas/src/source/types.ts` says that telemetry tick k is aligned with revision k (read at `53db46e`, not rechecked). The contradiction in `canvas/README.md` about relationships with a missing endpoint is gone at `14c2965`. Gone since the last evaluation: `engine/PERF.md` now says that the benchmarks load the tracked space without the local overlay (so the health chain is almost empty in H5 and H10, `bench-overlay-head6.txt`) and gives current test counts. The paper uses its own counts and says so.

**External.**
- Two captures from one cluster with a demo workload and a load generator. They are not a representative estate (a few hundred non-evidence entities). They have no `lost_events` and no forwarder markers in their direct sources, no pod name reuse (Q5 not evaluated) and no late-arriving Hubble history beyond what the replay handled. The VNet flow logs are 2 h of one cluster.
- The real captures are git-ignored and stay local, the cluster they came from no longer exists, and the space binding they need is a gitignored overlay. A third party can reproduce the replays of the fixtures and of the synthetic `estate` (both in the repository, with invented or scrubbed names) and the synthetic benchmarks, but not the real-capture figures, the ten-run H5 and H10 figures (their workload is the newest capture) or anything that needs Azure. Those rest on `paper/evidence/*.txt` and on the claim that the captures exist.
- No comparison with Feldera, Materialize, Memgraph or Neo4j (B15, B16), so the paper makes no claim of relative performance.

**Conclusion validity.**
- Statuses use the rule of Ch. 13 as written, with the scope clause of 13.6. Where a figure is far from its target (H9) or far inside it (H6, H2), a single run is still a single run. The three `supported` statuses (H5 engine part, H8, H10) are inside their targets (a factor of about 77 for H5 on SQLite for the records that made a revision, about 2,000 for all records in memory, about 2 for H8 on its worst run of any set, and about 5.7 for H10 with a seal on its worst run and 1.5 without one, under load), and each is for one configuration on a machine under load; none says anything about the Cosmos store or the reference hardware. H8 is the closest to its target. H7 has two different labels on the emulator from two sets, which says that the emulator figure is not a property of the design.
- Evidence that the engine matches the *paper* is limited to the listed tests. Where no test exists (T9, T10, T12, T13, provenance, a stack of lens links), the paper's claims stand as proofs only. Several statements (Proposition 1.8 on the survivor, Proposition 5.7 on refactoring, Proposition 5.9 on retention, Propositions 7.7 and 7.8 on the health model, Proposition 9.11 on evidence elements, Proposition 10.7 on live runs, Proposition 10.8 on live resume, Proposition 11.9 on restart, Proposition 11.10 on restart from a state seal, Proposition 12.10 on tags, the premises B1 to B3 of Theorem 6.2) are proof sketches with example tests or none. The health states on the second capture show the roll-up working and do not show that the cluster was unhealthy.
