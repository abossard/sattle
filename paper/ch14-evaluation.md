**Decision rule.** Chapter 13 (13.6) fixes the rule for hypotheses: a latency or size target is *supported* iff the statistic is at or below the target in every one of at least 10 independent runs, *refuted* iff the median run is above it, otherwise *inconclusive*. A status is stated for a configuration (store, machine, load): see 13.6 for how the rule is applied to a machine that is not the reference of Def. 13.4. A figure from fewer than ten runs is reported as *met in one run*, *not met in one run* or *not measured*. H5 (engine part, memory and SQLite), H8 (hot plane at the head revision) and H10 (restart from SQLite, with and without a state seal, at 1.2 h and 4.1 h of repeated capture) are `supported` in that sense. H7 has two labels on the Cosmos emulator from two sets of runs (`refuted` and `inconclusive`) and no status for a real account. Every other hypothesis is open (14.7).

## 14.2 The lab

- **Cluster.** An AKS cluster with Azure CNI powered by Cilium (it no longer exists: its resource group was deleted on 2026-11-10, not by this project, as set A reports; the two captures were taken before that), ACNS observability enabled for Hubble relay, Kubernetes 1.34, managed Prometheus (`lab/README.md`, `lab/CAPTURE-FORMAT.md`).
- **Workload.** Namespace `sattle-demo` with `frontend`, `api`, `db`, `loadgen` and an `intruder` that network policy denies (`lab/README.md`).
- **Capture tooling.** A recording script records, per source, NDJSON envelopes (`v`, `source`, `capturedAt`, `seq`, `key`, `meta`, `payload`). Sources captured: Azure Resource Graph, Kubernetes list and watch, Hubble flows, Prometheus range queries, Azure Monitor metrics, Activity Log backfill, VNet flow logs and Event Hubs mirrors (`lab/CAPTURE-FORMAT.md`). A forwarder publishes the same envelopes to an Event Hub for the live input (Ch. 10, 10.6).
- **Deployment.** The lab is an azd project rooted in `lab/` with hooks (Ch. 10, 10.5). The lab developers measured one real `azd up` (3 min 14 s) and one `azd down` (2 min 23 s) on the existing cluster, and the state after teardown against a baseline taken before (`lab/README.md`; not re-run by this paper, which cannot deploy to Azure). What teardown leaves behind: subscription deployment records, a soft-deleted storage account, ACNS leftovers in `kube-system`, and `advancedNetworking` reading `enabled: false` where it read `null`. A second `azd up` was not run.
- **Captures used.** Two. `20261110T105738Z` (`paper/evidence/replay-head6-105738.txt`): 245,345 records (243,979 in evaluation 1, before the Event Hubs diagnostic records were read), 235,858 Hubble flows over 725 s plus 522 VNet flow log records with 175,265 tuples, a capture span of 5,679 s. `20261110T140542Z` (`replay-head6-140542.txt`): 160,300 records, 146,759 Hubble records, 1,148 VNet flow log records, records from every source, a capture span of 1,017 s. No further capture can be taken while no cluster exists. Both captures are gitignored and stay local. The tracked space file binds the health model to invented names; the real workload arguments are in a gitignored overlay next to the captures (`lab/captures/space-overlay.sattle`), which `replay` and `serve` apply to a capture under `lab/captures`. This paper's runs used a copy of that overlay that matches the committed definitions.
- **VNet flow logs.** Tenant policy blocks public network access to the storage account. They are read through a short-lived pod in the cluster (`lab/README.md`, "Flow log blobs"). No `lost_events` records occurred in this capture.
- **Fixtures.** `lab/fixtures/` holds scrubbed samples with the same envelope. The engine tests replay them. They were rebuilt once from the first capture with a stricter scrubber, so their state digest changed (set A reports `a90c5be7…` before and `43917169…` after; this paper cites no fixture digest elsewhere). A second fixture is the **synthetic `estate`** (`engine/tests/Sattle.Tests/fixtures/estate`, written deterministically by `build-estate.py`, invented names only: cluster `synth`, namespace `shop`, services `web`, `api` and `db`, nodes `node-a` and `node-b`; 548 records from every source the engine reads) with a space file that binds the health model to those names, so that every operator of the canvas walkthroughs has data to produce output from (Ch. 12, 12.4; `estate-head4.txt`). The tracked space file binds these invented names, so the fixture is served with `sattle serve DIR --cluster synth` and no overlay. It is also the default source of the canvas (Ch. 12, 12.4).
- **Size.** The lab estate is small. After replay of the first capture the master graph has 15,819 entities and 5,537 relationships, of which 15,363 entities are evidence elements, kept for 24 h of logical time (Ch. 9, 9.6; without the retention rule 16,777 entities, 16,321 of them evidence elements); the other 456 entities are the estate itself. The reference estate of Ch. 11 has 750,000 entities. The lab proves the pipeline end to end, not scale.
- **Transport.** The forwarder was measured separately: about 125 events per second per throughput unit (Ch. 10, 10.6).

## 14.3 The system under test

- **Engine** (`engine/`, evaluation 12): a .NET 10 kernel with fifteen definition modules loaded from `.sattle` text (`paper/evidence/check-head9.txt` at evaluation 9, 0 errors and 0 warnings). The build treats warnings as errors (`engine/README.md`; this paper saw `sattle check` report 0 warnings and a successful build, not a compiler warning count).
- **Adapters:** Kubernetes (with the forwarder's markers), Hubble, Azure Resource Graph, Prometheus, Activity Log (management API and Event Hubs diagnostic records), AKS audit records, Azure Monitor metrics, VNet flow logs.
- **Language:** 19 of the 20 operator kinds (Ch. 5; `Delta` is missing), aggregates declared per context, parameters, levels written with the `lift` statement, whose mapping is a declared function or has a stated `images` policy (5.8), `lens` and `bijective` rules with backward edits (Ch. 8), identity merge with an order-independent survivor (Ch. 1), references for missing endpoints (Ch. 3).
- **Stores:** in-memory, SQLite and Cosmos DB behind one port, with chunked commits and a writer lease on Cosmos, a journal for restart from a store, a state seal that bounds the cost of that restart, a served history capped at 2,000 patches per graph and 20,000 events, and batched Cosmos writes (Ch. 10, 11; Cosmos against the emulator only).
- **Protocol version 3:** drafts, releases, preview, refactoring, value roles (on deployment health and on the health model), document projections, content tags, lineage in both directions, references alone, meter declarations and references in the static output (Ch. 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12).
- **Live input:** an Event Hubs reader that resumes from the journal (Ch. 10; not run here, its loop is tested with a fake source).
- **Examples:** the health model of Ch. 7, deployment health and its report (Ch. 2, 12), the service and capability levels (Ch. 5).
- **Canvas** (`canvas/`, the canvas of evaluation 11, Ch. 12; this paper's runs are at the canvas of evaluation 8): data, space, ontology and document views, the projection editor, default coloring from value roles, the default source from engine output, the abstraction highlight, reference stubs and collapsed supporting elements, and a `Lift` card.
- **Lab** (`lab/`, the lab of evaluation 6): the azd project of Ch. 10.
- **Tests.** Engine at evaluation 12, from set A: `Sattle.Tests` 670 of 670 in every category, and `Sattle.Differential` 149 of 149 against the independent reference evaluator. At evaluation 10: 639 of 639 in the full run with the Cosmos emulator (17 min 45 s under load). Engine at evaluation 9, this paper (`tests-head9.txt`): 0 failures of 463 tests in the default run (2 m 7 s here), of 96 in the slow category and of 20 in the capture category, 579 in all, without the Cosmos cases (set A reports 598 of 598 with the emulator at that commit, 19 more). Canvas (`canvas-head9.txt`): 643 tests in 33 files, 0 failures, against no engine; the canvas scripts ran against the engine at evaluation 7 (evaluation 8) and at evaluation 9 (evaluation 9).

What is not built is in the limits table of Ch. 15 (15.2).

## 14.5 Determinism and batch invariance

**T7 (replay determinism, Theorem 6.3).**
- A test replays the fixtures twice: two replays of the fixtures give the same state digest and, for every graph, pairwise equal patches and equal delta encodings. Class C.
- Two tests run on the real recordings: every capture present is replayed at chunk sizes 1, 50 and 100,000 with the replay defaults and the state digest, the number of identity conflicts (0) and the digest of every graph are compared. Class E. This paper ran the capture category with both captures linked into the export in evaluations 4 to 7 and 9, and all tests passed (18 tests, 20 from evaluation 7 on; `tests-head3.txt`, `tests-head4.txt`, `tests-head6.txt`, `tests-head7.txt`, `tests-head9.txt`). This paper's own replays at five sizes are below.

**T6 (batch invariance, Theorem 6.2).**
- A test with chunk sizes 1, 2, 3, 7, 50 and 100,000 against 2000: equal state digests, and the integrated output equals the final graph (the Quine invariant of O6.1). Class C on fixtures.
- **B1 and B2 have targeted tests:** one resource under two spellings of its id counts into one window under every batching (B1, three cases); a row for a window beyond the late horizon is dropped, counted and replaces nothing (B2, four cases); a row inside the horizon revises its window (the other side of B2). They pass in the default run. Each test fails when its fix is reverted (`mutation-head6.txt`, this paper's repeat of set B's check): with the groups of `acme.ops` keyed by the raw `resourceId`, the three B1 cases fail; with the drop of a row beyond the late horizon disabled in the engine, the four B2 cases fail and the two cases inside the horizon still pass. Class C. The suite had no such tests in evaluations 4 and 5 (found by searching `engine/tests/`); in that time only the capture tests guarded the two premises.
- **D17 resolved.** In evaluation 1 chunk size 50 gave a different digest on the first capture, with one differing Activity Log window (3 against 2). Two causes, both fixed (Ch. 6, 6.7). Since evaluation 2 (`replay-chunks-head.txt`) the default chunking (1,636 chunks), 50, 777 and 100,000 give the **same state digest** on `20261110T105738Z`, and the 4,595 master telemetry samples are the same.
- **D21 resolved.** In evaluation 2 chunk size 1 gave a different digest on the first capture (eight `Calls` relationships with a different endpoint id, identity counters 0 conflicts against 6; `chunk-1-defect.txt`). Merging two identities that denote one entity made the digests equal at five sizes, but the number of merges still depended on the chunk size (1 at 50 and above, 0 at 1; `replay-chunks-merge.txt`). With the survivor chosen as the smallest id minted for any key of the entity (Ch. 1, Proposition 1.8) the result is **one digest per capture at all five sizes** (default, 1, 50, 777, 100,000) with the same number of merges at every size (35 and 43) and 0 conflicts, in evaluations 4 to 7 and 9 (`replay-chunks-head3.txt`, `replay-chunks-head4.txt`, `replay-chunks-head6.txt`, `replay-chunks-head7.txt`, `replay-chunks-head9.txt`). At evaluation 7 to evaluation 10 the digests are `sha256:4df6bedf…8b775` on `20261110T105738Z` and `sha256:2a0b72c2…be93e` on `20261110T140542Z`. At evaluation 12 they are `sha256:cad8907e…89d4` and `sha256:6de23264…2370`: this paper replayed each capture once at the default chunking, and set B reports the same digest at chunk sizes 1, 50 and 100,000 with 0 conflicts, which neither set A nor this paper repeated. Chunking changes how many provisional identities are minted before the merge (16,722 at chunk size 1 against 17,564 at 100,000 on the first capture, evaluation 4), and the merge makes the result the same. The document of the report has one digest at chunk sizes 1 and 100,000 although the graph has 60 and 39 revisions (`document-head3.txt`, `document-head4.txt`, `document-head6.txt`).
- **Reading.** Theorem 6.2 holds on the graphs, telemetry and documents of both captures under five chunk sizes. Its premises B1 and B2 are covered by targeted tests and by the capture digests. B3 (identity independent of arrival order) holds on these two inputs, on the merge examples of the engine's tests and on one synthetic input in two arrival orders at four chunk sizes; Proposition 1.8 states it with a sketch. **No generator produces random key arrival orders**, so B3 is a tested premise and not a proved or generally tested one.

**What this does not cover.** Different interleavings of independent sources beyond the shuffled arrival of the live reproducibility tests (distance up to 40 places, three seeds, on the fixtures; one more on the newest capture). Repeating the same replay many times on a real capture. Different platforms (Q13). Determinism of a **Cosmos** run on a real capture: sets B and A report an equal digest on the emulator before and after the batched writes (Ch. 11, 11.12), which this paper did not repeat. A live run replayed from its recorded stream on the real Event Hubs reader (Proposition 10.7 is tested against a stand-in). The SQLite store was tested for seal digests and the replays ran in memory. The state digest of a repeated capture (the history of H10) is the same whether the run took seals or not (`sha256:833e7e6d…ec601` and `sha256:7aa3ac42…dcbc6`, set A without seals, this paper with a seal every 2,000 entries).

## 14.6 Theorems against tests

Every numbered statement of the paper has a status: proved, proved relative to a cited theorem, proved under a named premise, or tested only ([inventory.md](inventory.md)). Tests can support a proof, never replace it. They are of three kinds:

- **Seeded random tests.** A generator produces random cases from fixed seeds, 4,000 cases per property, so a run can be repeated exactly. They cover the algebra of changes and the versions of a graph (Ch. 3, 4), with one caveat: the generator keeps only changes that the prototype itself accepts, so a wrong rejection cannot be seen.
- **Comparison with an independent implementation.** A second, simple implementation computes every result from scratch, from the definitions of this paper. The prototype's step-by-step result is compared with it on generated inputs, under every way of cutting the input into batches and with reordering inside a batch. This tests Theorems 6.1 and 6.2 per operator kind; `Lift` and `Fixpoint` are not covered independently, because the simple implementation uses the prototype's own translation of them.
- **Example tests** on small inputs and on the two recordings.

Which test exercises which statement is listed in the appendix (Artifact, A.8).

## 14.9 Threats to validity

**Construct.**
- The hypotheses of Ch. 13 name Azure hardware and Cosmos. The measurements are on a laptop-class machine with in-memory and SQLite stores and, for the Cosmos store, the vNext emulator, which reports a request charge of 1 and is not a latency model of a real account. A pass here is not a pass there, and `H3`, `H7`, `H13` cannot be confirmed without a real Cosmos account.
- H4 and H12 were measured with one `Advance` call that includes two graphs. The hypothesis concerns a per-hop latency over a stack of at least three projections.
- The synthetic benchmark workload has 5,000 pair series (`engine/PERF.md`). The reference estate has about 240,000 flow pairs.

**Internal.**
- Mostly one run per figure, on a machine that was shared with other work sessions in the case of the canvas. `engine/PERF.md` reports no variance for the single-run figures; the ten-run figures (H5, H7, H8, H10) report median, minimum, maximum and an order-statistic interval. **A machine with no other load was not available to set A, set B or this paper**: set A's ten-run figures were taken at a load average of 25 to 106 at evaluation 4 and 25 to 75 at evaluation 5 (recorded per run in `perf-*-orch*.txt`), set B's at 14 to 115, and the runs of this paper at 27 to 91 at evaluation 4, 16 to 70 at evaluation 5 (one set of three restarts at 100 to 239) and 16 to 82 at evaluation 6. The label a figure gets is therefore scoped to that load (Ch. 13, 13.6). For an upper-bound target, load can only raise latencies, so a pass under load holds a fortiori and a failure under load is weak evidence; the one failure, H7 on the emulator, is also on a store that is not the target. The estate run is one run of four windows.
- The property tests are by the same authors as the proofs. The generators are seeded and the properties test the engine types, not an independent implementation. Only the kernel has mutation tests.
- Several theorem statements were checked on fixtures and on one real capture, not on randomly generated projections (T5, 6.1; 5.2).
- Wall times of replays and test runs are single runs on a laptop that other contributors were using, so they are not performance figures (default engine run of 456 tests 1 m 52 s at evaluation 7; 403 tests in 2 m 6 s, 96 slow tests in 5 m 4 s and 18 capture tests in 3 m 52 s at evaluation 6, at load 45 to 90). Evaluation 1 replayed with a Debug build, every later one with a Release build. The capture-category tests need the two real captures linked into the export; in evaluations 1 to 3 they were not, and the tests that use a capture returned early.
- The investigation of D21 compared exported snapshots and telemetry with `jq` and did not instrument the engine, so its first cause was a reading of counters. That the merge removes the mismatch is a measurement on two inputs, and that the order-independent survivor removes the dependence in general is a sketch (Proposition 1.8) with one synthetic test and two captures. The causes of D17 were found by set B, not by this paper. The unit tests of B1 and B2 were absent from the suite in evaluations 4 and 5; a regression of the D17 causes was caught only by the capture tests in that time, and only on the shapes in those captures.
- From evaluation 10 on the paper's numbers at the current commit are set A's and not this paper's: the people who direct the work also verify it, and this paper's check is a reading of code and tests. The evidence files in `paper/evidence/` end at evaluation 9.
- Canvas and engine were evaluated at different times. The canvas tests, the walkthroughs on the `estate` and the accessibility script are at the canvas of evaluation 8 against the engines of evaluation 7 and evaluation 9. The canvas at the canvas of evaluation 11, with its `Lift` card, its regenerated default source and the references route, was checked in set A and not by this paper. The walkthrough row on the newest real capture is in evaluation 6 and was not repeated. What the canvas shows on the served newest capture (589 stubs, 143 collapsed evidence elements, the propagated failure) was seen in a browser in set A and is not asserted by a script.
- Every evaluation is of a commit, not of the working tree, which held uncommitted changes of other contributors. The real-capture runs depend on a gitignored overlay file that only this machine has and that other contributors edit; this paper used a copy that matches the committed definitions (the state digests confirm the copy), after one batch of runs in evaluation 6 failed to load with the shared file and was repeated.
- The lab figures (azd times, state after teardown) are the lab developers' own measurements. This paper did not deploy to Azure and cannot repeat them, and nobody can at present: the cluster no longer exists.
- **Disagreements between the repository's documents and its code** as last checked by this paper (engine documents at evaluation 9, canvas README at the canvas of evaluation 11). (1) `engine/PERF.md` gives 578 tests for the full run without the emulator; this paper counts 579 as the sum of its three runs (463, 96 and 20), which agrees with set A's 598 with the emulator less the 19 Cosmos cases. The default count, 463, agrees. (2) A comment in the viewer's source says that telemetry tick k is aligned with revision k (read at the canvas of evaluation 8, not rechecked). The contradiction in `canvas/README.md` about relationships with a missing endpoint is gone at the canvas of evaluation 11. Gone since the last evaluation: `engine/PERF.md` now says that the benchmarks load the tracked space without the local overlay (so the health chain is almost empty in H5 and H10, `bench-overlay-head6.txt`) and gives current test counts. The paper uses its own counts and says so.

**External.**
- Two captures from one cluster with a demo workload and a load generator. They are not a representative estate (a few hundred non-evidence entities). They have no `lost_events` and no forwarder markers in their direct sources, no pod name reuse (Q5 not evaluated) and no late-arriving Hubble history beyond what the replay handled. The VNet flow logs are 2 h of one cluster.
- The real captures are git-ignored and stay local, the cluster they came from no longer exists, and the space binding they need is a gitignored overlay. A third party can reproduce the replays of the fixtures and of the synthetic `estate` (both in the repository, with invented or scrubbed names) and the synthetic benchmarks, but not the real-capture figures, the ten-run H5 and H10 figures (their workload is the newest capture) or anything that needs Azure. Those rest on `paper/evidence/*.txt` and on the claim that the captures exist.
- No comparison with Feldera, Materialize, Memgraph or Neo4j (B15, B16), so the paper makes no claim of relative performance.

**Conclusion validity.**
- Statuses use the rule of Ch. 13 as written, with the scope clause of 13.6. Where a figure is far from its target (H9) or far inside it (H6, H2), a single run is still a single run. The three `supported` statuses (H5 engine part, H8, H10) are inside their targets (a factor of about 77 for H5 on SQLite for the records that made a revision, about 2,000 for all records in memory, about 2 for H8 on its worst run of any set, and about 5.7 for H10 with a seal on its worst run and 1.5 without one, under load), and each is for one configuration on a machine under load; none says anything about the Cosmos store or the reference hardware. H8 is the closest to its target. H7 has two different labels on the emulator from two sets, which says that the emulator figure is not a property of the design.
- Evidence that the engine matches the *paper* is limited to the listed tests. Where no test exists (T9, T10, T12, T13, provenance, a stack of lens links), the paper's claims stand as proofs only. Several statements (Proposition 1.8 on the survivor, Proposition 5.7 on refactoring, Proposition 5.9 on retention, Propositions 7.7 and 7.8 on the health model, Proposition 9.11 on evidence elements, Proposition 10.7 on live runs, Proposition 10.8 on live resume, Proposition 11.9 on restart, Proposition 11.10 on restart from a state seal, Proposition 12.10 on tags, the premises B1 to B3 of Theorem 6.2) are proof sketches with example tests or none. The health states on the second capture show the roll-up working and do not show that the cluster was unhealthy.
